Hatsa – Game Rules

English version

1Objective of the Game

In Hatsa, players try to predict exactly how many tricks they will take each round. The more often your prediction is correct, the more points you score. The player with the highest total score wins the game.

2Scorecard and Tracking Points

Before the game starts, create a scorecard to track points for all players.

  • Write player names across the top.
  • List rounds vertically (from the lowest round up to the highest and back down).
  • After each round, record each player’s score for that round.
  • Add up all rounds at the end to determine the winner.
Tip: If you use a “printable score sheet” page, you only need to write down Prediction + Points per round.

3Cards and Card Values

The Hatsa deck consists of multiple colours. Each colour contains cards with values 1 through 9 and E, D, C, B, A. A is the highest card. The highest card in the led suit wins the trick, unless a Hatsa card is played – a Hatsa card always beats other colours.

4Setup

  1. Choose a dealer (for example, rock-paper-scissors).
  2. Shuffle the deck.
  3. Decide how many rounds you want to play before starting.
  4. Rounds progress from a low number of cards to a high number and then back down again (for example: 1 → 10 → 1).
  5. Deal the correct number of cards for the round.
  6. Each player starts the game with one action card.

5Round Structure

  1. Dealing: players receive their cards.
  2. Determine Hatsa card: flip the top card of the deck; its colour becomes the Hatsa card colour for the round.
  3. Action card phase: players may use an action card.
  4. Predicting: countdown 3-2-1 and reveal predictions using fingers.
  5. Playing tricks: follow suit if possible; the highest card in the led suit or the highest Hatsa card wins.

6Action Cards

Each player may hold at most one action card at any time. Action cards are single-use special abilities that may only be used before predicting.

You start the game with one action card. When you reach the highest round number (for example 10 in a 1–10–1 game), each player receives a new action card.

If you are holding an action card, you must use it before the next action card is handed out. Unused action cards may not be carried over beyond that point.

During the action card phase:

  • The player to the dealer’s left goes first.
  • In clockwise order, players decide whether to use their action card.
  • Using the action card is optional (unless it would otherwise expire).
  • When an action card is used, resolve the effect immediately and remove it from the game.

The effect of each action card is written on the card itself.

7How to Predict

Analyse your hand based on high cards, Hatsa cards, and distribution across suits. Decide how many tricks you realistically expect to take and how much risk you want to take.

8Scoring

Correct prediction:

  • 10 points
  • +5 points per trick taken

Incorrect prediction:

  • No bonus
  • -5 points per trick difference

Examples:

  • Predicted 3, took 2 → -5 points
  • Predicted 5, took 2 → -15 points

9End of the Game

After the final round, all players add up their points. The player with the highest total score wins.
